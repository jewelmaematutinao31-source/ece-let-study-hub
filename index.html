🧬 Lesson 1 — Fertilization & Prenatal Development

📖

📌 What is prenatal development?

Prenatal development refers to the development of a baby from fertilization until birth.

🧬 Fertilization

Fertilization happens when a sperm cell joins with an ovum (egg cell). This produces a zygote.

Easy example: Sperm + ovum → zygote.

🪜 Main prenatal periods

  • Germinal Period — begins at fertilization and includes the early cell divisions and implantation.
  • Embryonic Period — approximately the third through eighth week after fertilization; major structures begin developing.
  • Fetal Period — begins around the ninth week after fertilization and continues until birth.
Example:
Fertilization → zygote → embryo → fetus → birth.

🌱 Implantation

Implantation is when the developing organism attaches to the lining of the uterus.

🏫 Classroom connection: A teacher may explain prenatal development using a simple timeline or picture sequence showing how development progresses before birth.
🔑 LET clues:
Sperm + ovum = fertilization
Fertilization product = zygote
Early development = germinal
Major structures developing = embryonic
Growth and maturation before birth = fetal
🧠 Memory aid:
Z-E-F → Zygote → Embryo → Fetus.

🤰 Lesson 2 — Prenatal Influences

📖

📌 What affects prenatal development?

Prenatal development can be influenced by both genetic and environmental factors.

⚠️ Teratogens

A teratogen is an environmental substance, condition, or factor that can negatively affect prenatal development.

Easy example: Certain harmful substances, infections, or environmental exposures can interfere with prenatal development.

🥗 Maternal nutrition

Adequate nutrition supports healthy prenatal growth and development.

❤️ Maternal health

A mother's health and medical conditions can influence prenatal development.

⏰ Critical periods

Some stages of prenatal development are especially sensitive because important organs and structures are developing.

🏫 Classroom connection: Early childhood educators should understand that children's development can be affected by biological and environmental factors and should avoid blaming families for developmental differences.
🔑 LET clue: If the question describes a harmful environmental influence during pregnancy, think teratogen.
🧠 Memory aid: Teratogen = prenatal danger.

👶 Lesson 3 — Birth & Newborn Development

📖

📌 Newborn period

The newborn period refers to the earliest period of life after birth.

👄 Rooting Reflex

When the baby's cheek or mouth area is touched, the baby turns toward the stimulus and may open the mouth.

Example: A baby's cheek is gently touched and the baby turns toward the touch looking for a source of feeding.

🍼 Sucking Reflex

A newborn naturally sucks when something touches the mouth.

😲 Moro Reflex

A sudden stimulus can cause the baby to extend the arms, followed by bringing them inward.

✋ Grasping Reflex

A baby may close the fingers when the palm is touched.

🏫 Classroom connection: Infant caregivers should respond appropriately to infants' signals and provide safe, responsive care.
🔑 LET clues:
Turns toward touch near mouth → rooting
Sucks → sucking
Startles and extends arms → Moro
Closes fingers around touch → grasping

🍼 Lesson 4 — Infancy & Toddlerhood

📖

🏃 Physical development

Infants and toddlers gradually gain control of their bodies and develop gross- and fine-motor skills.

Typical sequence:
Head control → sitting → crawling → standing → walking.

🧠 Cognitive development

Infants learn through sensory experiences and physical interaction with their environment.

👀 Object permanence

Object permanence is the understanding that an object continues to exist even when it cannot currently be seen.

Example: A baby looks for a toy after an adult hides it under a blanket.

🗣️ Language development

Language develops from early sounds and gestures toward meaningful words and increasing vocabulary.

Crying → cooing → babbling → gestures → first words → vocabulary growth.

❤️ Social-emotional development

Consistent and responsive caregiving supports emotional security and relationships.

🏫 Classroom connection: Teachers can provide safe opportunities for infants and toddlers to explore, communicate, move, and interact with responsive adults.
🔑 LET clue: "The child knows the toy is still there even though it is hidden" → object permanence.

🧸 Lesson 5 — Preschool Development

📖

🏃 Physical development

Preschool children generally become more coordinated and develop greater balance, strength, and fine-motor control.

Example: A preschooler uses scissors, draws shapes, climbs, runs, and jumps.

🧠 Cognitive development

Preschoolers are curious, imaginative, increasingly capable of using symbols, and interested in understanding how things work.

🎭 Pretend play

Children may use objects, actions, or roles to represent something else.

Example: A child uses a block as a pretend phone.

🗣️ Language development

Vocabulary, sentence length, storytelling, and conversational abilities develop rapidly during the preschool years.

❤️ Social development

Children begin developing friendships, cooperation, self-control, and understanding of social rules.

🏫 Classroom connection: Provide opportunities for pretend play, conversation, cooperative activities, movement, art, and exploration.
🔑 LET clue: Do not expect every preschooler to develop at exactly the same speed. Individual differences are normal.

🧠 Lesson 6 — Developmental Theorists

📖

🧠 Jean Piaget — Cognitive Development

Piaget focused on how children actively construct knowledge and how their thinking changes as they develop.

1️⃣ Sensorimotor

Birth to about 2 years. Children learn mainly through senses and physical actions.

Example: A baby shakes a rattle and learns that shaking it produces a sound.

2️⃣ Preoperational

About 2–7 years. Children develop symbolic thinking but their thinking is not yet fully logical.

Example: A child pretends a banana is a telephone.

3️⃣ Concrete Operational

About 7–11 years. Children become more capable of logical thinking about concrete objects and events.

Example: A child understands that the amount of water stays the same even when it is poured into a differently shaped glass.

4️⃣ Formal Operational

About 11 years and beyond. Abstract and hypothetical reasoning becomes more developed.

Example: A teenager considers several possible outcomes of a hypothetical situation.
🔑 Piaget LET clues:
Thinking/cognition → Piaget
Object permanence → Sensorimotor
Symbolic/pretend thinking → Preoperational
Conservation → Concrete operational
Abstract/hypothetical reasoning → Formal operational
🧠 Memory: S-P-C-F
Sensorimotor → Preoperational → Concrete → Formal

❤️ Erik Erikson — Psychosocial Development

Erikson focused on psychosocial development and the major conflicts or challenges people encounter throughout life.

1. Trust vs. Mistrust

Infancy. Children develop trust when caregivers respond consistently to their needs.

Example: A baby learns that crying brings a caregiver who provides comfort and care.

2. Autonomy vs. Shame & Doubt

Toddlerhood. Children want to do things independently.

Example: A toddler says, "I do it!" while trying to feed or dress themselves.

3. Initiative vs. Guilt

Preschool years. Children develop initiative by planning, creating, pretending, and taking action.

Example: A preschooler creates a pretend restaurant and assigns roles to classmates.

4. Industry vs. Inferiority

School age. Children develop a sense of competence through learning and accomplishing tasks.

Example: A child practices writing and feels proud after completing a difficult assignment.

5. Identity vs. Role Confusion

Adolescence. Young people explore values, interests, beliefs, and possible future roles.

Example: A teenager explores different careers and interests.

6. Intimacy vs. Isolation

Young adulthood. Developing meaningful relationships is an important task.

7. Generativity vs. Stagnation

Adulthood. Adults may contribute to others and the next generation.

Example: An adult mentors younger people.

8. Integrity vs. Despair

Later adulthood. People reflect on their lives and whether they view their life as meaningful.

🔑 Erikson LET clues:
Baby → Trust
Toddler → Autonomy
Preschool → Initiative
School age → Industry
Adolescent → Identity
🧠 Easy memory:
T-A-I-I-I-I-G-I
Trust → Autonomy → Initiative → Industry → Identity → Intimacy → Generativity → Integrity

👩‍🏫 Lev Vygotsky — Sociocultural Theory

Vygotsky emphasized that children's learning is strongly influenced by social interaction, culture, language, and guidance from others.

🤝 Social Interaction

Children learn through interaction with teachers, adults, and peers.

Example: A child learns a new word while talking with a teacher.

📍 Zone of Proximal Development (ZPD)

The ZPD is the range between what a learner can do independently and what the learner can do with appropriate assistance.

Example: A child cannot complete a puzzle alone but can finish it when the teacher gives helpful guidance.

🪜 Scaffolding

Scaffolding means providing temporary support that helps a learner accomplish a task and gradually reducing that support as the learner becomes more capable.

Example: A teacher gives hints while a child learns to write their name, then gradually gives fewer hints.

⭐ More Knowledgeable Other (MKO)

The MKO is someone who has more knowledge or skill in a particular task and can help the learner.

Example: A teacher or more-skilled peer helps a child learn a new activity.
🔑 Vygotsky clues:
Social/cultural learning → Vygotsky
Can do with help but not alone → ZPD
Temporary support → Scaffolding
More knowledgeable helper → MKO

❤️ John Bowlby — Attachment

Bowlby emphasized the importance of attachment between children and caregivers.

Example: A baby seeks comfort from a familiar caregiver when distressed.
🔑 LET clue: Attachment relationship → Bowlby

⚖️ Lawrence Kohlberg — Moral Development

Kohlberg focused on how moral reasoning develops.

Example: A child may initially follow rules because of consequences, while more advanced reasoning considers broader principles.
🔑 LET clue: Moral reasoning → Kohlberg

🌍 Urie Bronfenbrenner — Ecological Systems

Bronfenbrenner emphasized that development occurs within interconnected environmental systems.

Example: A child's development may be influenced by family, school, community, culture, and broader social conditions.
🔑 LET clue: Development influenced by layers of environment → Bronfenbrenner.

🎨 Lesson 7 — Learning Through Play

📖

📌 Why is play important?

Play provides children with opportunities to explore, practice skills, communicate, solve problems, express ideas, and interact with others.

🧩 Types of social play

  • Solitary play — child plays alone.
  • Parallel play — children play beside each other but do not yet meaningfully interact.
  • Associative play — children interact and may share materials but have limited common goals.
  • Cooperative play — children play together toward a shared goal.
Example:
Two children sitting beside each other using separate blocks → parallel play.

Four children building one pretend house together → cooperative play.

🎯 What can play develop?

  • Problem-solving
  • Language
  • Creativity
  • Social skills
  • Physical skills
  • Emotional expression
🏫 Teacher role: Provide safe, interesting materials and observe, guide, question, model, and support children's play without unnecessarily taking control of it.
🔑 LET clue: In ECE, play is not simply "free time." It can be an important context for learning and development.

📈 Lesson 8 — Developmental Milestones

📖

📌 What are developmental milestones?

Developmental milestones are skills or behaviors that children commonly develop as they grow.

1️⃣ Physical / Motor

Includes gross-motor and fine-motor development.

Example: Running is a gross-motor skill. Using crayons to make marks involves fine-motor development.

2️⃣ Cognitive

Involves thinking, learning, memory, reasoning, and problem-solving.

Example: A child figures out how to fit a puzzle piece into the correct space.

3️⃣ Language / Communication

Includes understanding and expressing language.

Example: A child follows a simple instruction and later uses words to communicate needs.

4️⃣ Social-Emotional

Includes relationships, emotional expression, self-regulation, and social interaction.

Example: A child begins waiting for a turn and comforting a peer.
🏫 Classroom connection: Teachers observe children's development across multiple domains and provide appropriate experiences rather than expecting every child to develop at exactly the same rate.
🔑 LET strategy:
First identify the domain described in the question: physical, cognitive, language, or social-emotional. Then determine the developmental concept.
🧠 Memory: P-C-L-S
Physical → Cognitive → Language → Social-emotional
