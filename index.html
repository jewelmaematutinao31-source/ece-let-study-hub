Lesson 1

🧬 Fertilization & Prenatal Development

Learn what happens from fertilization until birth.

1. Fertilization

Fertilization happens when a sperm cell joins with an ovum, or egg cell.

Sperm + Ovum → Zygote

The single cell formed after fertilization is called a zygote.

2. Zygote

The zygote is the earliest stage after fertilization. It begins dividing into more cells.

3. Embryo

The developing organism is called an embryo during the embryonic period, approximately from the third week through the eighth week after fertilization.

4. Fetus

Beginning around the ninth week after fertilization, the developing organism is generally called a fetus. Growth and maturation continue until birth.

Prenatal Periods

Germinal Period
Fertilization to approximately 2 weeks. Cell division occurs and implantation takes place.
Embryonic Period
Approximately 2 to 8 weeks. Major organs and body structures begin developing.
Fetal Period
Approximately 9 weeks after fertilization until birth. Growth and maturation continue.

Remember This Order

Fertilization → Zygote → Embryo → Fetus → Birth

Implantation

Implantation occurs when the developing blastocyst attaches to the lining of the uterus.

Lesson 2

🤰 Prenatal Influences

Learn about factors that can influence development before birth.

Teratogens

A teratogen is an environmental agent that can negatively affect prenatal development.

Examples may include alcohol, certain drugs, some infections, radiation, and certain toxic environmental substances.

Maternal Nutrition

Adequate nutrition supports healthy prenatal growth and development.

Maternal Health

Health conditions, infections, medications, nutritional status, and environmental exposures can influence prenatal development.

Critical Periods

Some periods of prenatal development are especially sensitive because important organs and structures are developing rapidly.

LET Tip: Remember that prenatal development can be influenced by genetic and environmental factors.
Lesson 3

👶 Birth & Newborn Development

Important concepts about birth and the newborn period.

Newborn Period

The newborn period is the earliest period of life after birth.

Important Newborn Reflexes

Rooting Reflex
The baby turns toward a touch near the cheek or mouth.
Sucking Reflex
The baby automatically sucks when something touches the mouth.
Moro Reflex
A startle response that may involve extending and then bringing the arms inward.
Grasping Reflex
The baby closes the fingers when an object touches the palm.

Newborn Development

  • Rapid physical changes occur.
  • Sensory abilities help babies respond to their environment.
  • Babies communicate through crying, facial expressions, and movements.
  • Responsive caregiving supports early development.
Lesson 4

🍼 Infancy & Toddlerhood

Understand physical, cognitive, language, and social-emotional development.

Physical Development

Infants gradually gain control over their bodies. Motor development generally progresses from simpler movements toward increasingly coordinated movements.

Head control → Sitting → Crawling → Standing → Walking

Cognitive Development

Infants learn through sensory experiences and physical actions. They explore objects and their environment.

Object Permanence

Object permanence is the understanding that an object continues to exist even when it is not currently visible.

Language Development

  • Crying
  • Cooing
  • Babbling
  • Gestures
  • First words
  • Increasing vocabulary

Social-Emotional Development

Consistent and responsive caregiving helps children develop emotional security and relationships.

Lesson 5

🧸 Preschool Development

Review development during the preschool years.

Physical Development

Preschool children generally develop greater coordination, balance, strength, and fine-motor control.

Cognitive Development

Children become increasingly curious and use language, imagination, symbols, and pretend play.

Language

Vocabulary and sentence complexity generally increase rapidly during the preschool years.

Social Development

Children develop friendships, cooperation, self-control, and an understanding of social rules.

Remember: Children do not all develop at exactly the same rate. Developmental milestones are useful guides.
Lesson 6

🧠 Developmental Theorists

Learn major theorists commonly associated with child development.

Jean Piaget

Cognitive Development
Focused on how children actively construct knowledge through interaction with their environment.

Four major stages:

  • Sensorimotor
  • Preoperational
  • Concrete operational
  • Formal operational

Erik Erikson

Psychosocial Development
Described development through psychosocial conflicts across the lifespan.

Lev Vygotsky

Sociocultural Theory
Emphasized social interaction, language, culture, and guided learning.

John Bowlby

Attachment Theory
Emphasized the importance of early emotional bonds between children and caregivers.

Lawrence Kohlberg

Moral Development
Studied the development of moral reasoning.

Urie Bronfenbrenner

Ecological Systems Theory
Examined how different environmental systems influence human development.
Lesson 7

🎨 Learning Through Play

Understand why play is important in early childhood.

Why Children Play

  • Develops problem-solving skills
  • Supports language development
  • Encourages creativity
  • Builds social skills
  • Supports physical development
  • Allows children to practice ideas

Types of Play

Solitary Play
A child plays independently.
Parallel Play
Children play beside one another with limited interaction.
Associative Play
Children interact or share materials but do not necessarily organize their play around one common goal.
Cooperative Play
Children play together toward a shared goal.

Teacher's Role

An early childhood teacher can provide safe materials, observe children, ask meaningful questions, encourage exploration, and provide appropriate support.

Lesson 8

📈 Developmental Milestones

Review the major areas of child development.

Four Major Developmental Domains

1. Physical / Motor
Movement, coordination, strength, and physical growth.
2. Cognitive
Thinking, memory, attention, reasoning, and problem solving.
3. Language / Communication
Understanding and using language and communicating with others.
4. Social-Emotional
Relationships, emotions, self-awareness, and social interaction.

Remember

Development is interconnected. A child may develop at different rates in different areas.

LET Tip: When you see a developmental question, identify the domain first: physical, cognitive, language, or social-emotional.
