Lesson 1

🧬 Fertilization & Prenatal Development

Learn what happens from fertilization until birth.

1. Fertilization

Fertilization happens when a sperm cell joins with an ovum (egg cell).

Sperm + Ovum → Zygote

The resulting cell is called a zygote.

2. Zygote

The zygote is the first stage after fertilization. It begins dividing into more cells.

3. Prenatal Periods

Germinal Period
Fertilization to approximately 2 weeks. The fertilized egg divides and travels toward the uterus.
Embryonic Period
Approximately 2 to 8 weeks. Major organs and body structures begin developing.
Fetal Period
Approximately 9 weeks until birth. Growth and maturation of body systems continue.

Remember This Order

Fertilization → Zygote → Embryo → Fetus → Birth

Implantation

Implantation occurs when the developing conceptus attaches to the lining of the uterus.

Lesson 2

🤰 Prenatal Influences

Learn about factors that can influence development before birth.

Teratogens

A teratogen is an environmental agent that can negatively affect prenatal development.

Examples can include alcohol, certain drugs, some infections, radiation, and certain toxic environmental substances.

Maternal Nutrition

Adequate nutrition supports healthy prenatal growth and development.

Maternal Health

A pregnant person's health, infections, medications, stress, and exposure to harmful substances may influence prenatal development.

Critical Periods

Some periods of prenatal development are especially important because particular organs and structures are developing rapidly.

LET Tip: When studying prenatal development, remember: nutrition + health + environment can all influence the developing baby.
Lesson 3

👶 Birth & Newborn Development

Important concepts about birth and the newborn period.

Newborn Period

The newborn period is the earliest period of life after birth.

Reflexes

Rooting Reflex
The baby turns toward a touch near the mouth or cheek.
Sucking Reflex
Helps the baby suck when something touches the mouth.
Moro Reflex
A startle response involving movements of the arms and body.
Grasping Reflex
The baby closes the fingers around an object placed in the palm.

Newborn Development

  • Rapid physical growth occurs.
  • Senses begin adapting to the environment.
  • Babies communicate through crying and other behaviors.
  • Early caregiver interaction supports development.
Lesson 4

🍼 Infancy & Toddlerhood

Understand physical, cognitive, language, and social-emotional development.

Physical Development

Infants gradually gain control over their bodies, progressing from simpler movements to more coordinated motor skills.

Head control → sitting → crawling → standing → walking

Cognitive Development

Infants learn through their senses and actions. They explore objects, people, and their environment.

Object Permanence

Object permanence is the understanding that an object continues to exist even when it cannot currently be seen.

Language Development

  • Crying and cooing
  • Babbling
  • Gestures
  • First words
  • Increasing vocabulary

Social-Emotional Development

Responsive and consistent caregivers help children develop trust, security, and emotional regulation.

Lesson 5

🧸 Preschool Development

Review development during the preschool years.

Physical Development

Preschool children generally develop better balance, coordination, strength, and fine-motor control.

Cognitive Development

Children become increasingly curious and use symbols, language, imagination, and pretend play.

Language

Vocabulary and sentence complexity increase rapidly during the preschool years.

Social Development

Children begin developing friendships, cooperation, self-control, and an understanding of social rules.

Important: Development happens at different rates. Milestones are useful guides, not exact deadlines for every child.
Lesson 6

🧠 Developmental Theorists

Learn the major theorists commonly associated with child development.

Jean Piaget

Cognitive Development
Focused on how children actively construct knowledge.

Four major stages:

  • Sensorimotor
  • Preoperational
  • Concrete operational
  • Formal operational

Erik Erikson

Psychosocial Development
Described development through psychosocial conflicts across the lifespan.

Lev Vygotsky

Sociocultural Theory
Emphasized social interaction, language, and cultural context in learning.

John Bowlby

Attachment Theory
Emphasized the importance of early emotional bonds between children and caregivers.

Lawrence Kohlberg

Moral Development
Studied the development of moral reasoning.

Urie Bronfenbrenner

Ecological Systems Theory
Examined how different environmental systems influence development.
Lesson 7

🎨 Learning Through Play

Understand why play is important in early childhood.

Why Children Play

  • Develops problem-solving skills
  • Supports language development
  • Encourages creativity
  • Builds social skills
  • Supports physical development
  • Allows children to practice new ideas

Types of Play

Solitary Play
A child plays independently.
Parallel Play
Children play beside each other but have limited interaction.
Associative Play
Children interact and share materials but may not have a common organized goal.
Cooperative Play
Children work or play together toward a shared goal.

Teacher's Role

An early childhood teacher can provide safe materials, observe children, ask questions, encourage exploration, and scaffold learning.

Lesson 8

📈 Developmental Milestones

Review major areas of child development.

Four Major Developmental Domains

1. Physical / Motor
Movement, coordination, strength, and body development.
2. Cognitive
Thinking, memory, attention, reasoning, and problem solving.
3. Language / Communication
Understanding and using language and other forms of communication.
4. Social-Emotional
Relationships, emotions, self-awareness, and social interaction.

Remember

Development is interconnected. A child may be developing rapidly in one area while progressing more gradually in another.

LET Tip: When answering a developmental question, identify which domain the behavior belongs to: physical, cognitive, language, or social-emotional.
